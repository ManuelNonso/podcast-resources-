Part One

Name

You cannot dispute a verdict you have never clearly read. Part One surfaces the specific labels currently running your life — across all four areas — and begins to make the invisible visible. The naming is where the wrong diagnosis loses its first layer of power.

The NHS Parallel

In the NHS story, the system was completely confident. The documentation was official. The letter, the text, the consultant call — all of it had the weight of institutional authority. And for a moment, even with absolute certainty that the result could not be mine, the confidence of the system created doubt. That is exactly what powerful labels do. They survive not because they are accurate — but because they are confident. The next part of this worksheet is about applying precision to the confidence. And watching what happens when you do.

Part Two

Trace

Every label has an origin. Every verdict was issued by someone, in a specific context, at a specific moment — and then accepted as a permanent truth about a person rather than a time-bound observation about a situation. Part Two traces the label to its source.

2
3
The Origin Trace
Locating the specific moment the label was issued — and by whom.

Use the label you named in Exercise 2 for all exercises in Part Two. You are now going to trace it back — through the layers of time and context — to the specific origin. This is not about finding someone to blame. It is about understanding the basis for the verdict, so you can assess whether that basis actually had the authority to make the label true.

🎯 Layer One — Who Issued This?

Who issued this label? Not necessarily the person who said it — the person, system, or experience whose verdict became the foundation of this diagnosis. It may have been stated explicitly. It may have been implied, ambient, communicated through behaviour rather than words. Who was the primary source?

Take time with this. The label may have multiple contributors — but there is usually one primary origin. Go for that one first.
🎯 Layer Two — What Was the Test?

What was the experience or evidence that was supposed to justify this label — the "test" that generated the result? How specific was it? How representative was it of the full range of who you are and what you are capable of? Was it a single performance in a single context — or something broader?

Most labels are generalised from a very specific data point. A single piece of work. One bad performance in one context. One relationship that ended. The label turned a specific, bounded experience into a universal statement about a person. Name the specific experience.
🎯 Layer Three — Were You Actually In the Room?

This is the question that ended the NHS monitoring programme. Was the test designed for you — for the full, actual, complete version of who you are? Or was it a system optimised for a different type of person, measuring a different kind of output, in a context that was not built to see what you actually bring?

The school system that called you inattentive was measuring compliance, not intelligence. The workplace that called you unsuitable was optimised for a template you were never built to fit. The relationship that called you too much was calibrated to a smaller version of intimacy than you were built for. Were you in the room — or was someone else's template in the room, with your name on it?

The Authority Audit
Testing the actual credential behind the label — not the perceived one.

The confidence of the delivery is not the same as the validity of the credential. The NHS had a letter. A text. A consultant on the phone. All of it was official. None of it was accurate. Apply the same precision to the authority behind your label.

Authority Audit — Answer Each Question for the Person or System Who Issued Your Label
What specific expertise did this person or system have in assessing this particular quality in this particular kind of person?Not general expertise — specific expertise for this assessment.
What information did they have access to — and what were they unable to see?What did they not know about you that would have changed the verdict?
What was their own ceiling at the time — and is it possible the verdict reflected their limitations rather than yours?People diagnose from where they stand.
Has their verdict been contradicted — by your own evidence, by others, by subsequent results?What has happened since that directly contradicts the label?
If you applied the same level of scrutiny to this verdict that a court would apply to disputed evidence — would it hold?Or does the confidence simply do the work the evidence cannot?

The Oprah Parallel

The producer who told Oprah Winfrey she was "unfit for television news" had genuine expertise in television news. The credential was real. The confidence was real. The authority was real in the context of that specific format, that specific role, that specific moment. But the verdict was not about Oprah Winfrey's total capacity for television — it was about her fit for one specific format, evaluated by one person, at one moment in her development. The label generalised a specific verdict into a universal one. And she went on to redefine the entire medium. The authority was real. The scope of the verdict was catastrophically wrong.

Part Three

Dispute

The label has been named. The origin has been traced. The authority has been audited. Part Three is where the dispute happens — not as an emotional rejection of an uncomfortable truth, but as a precise, evidence-based challenge to a verdict that does not hold under examination.

The Counter-Diagnosis
Building the evidence-based alternative to the wrong verdict.

The wrong diagnosis cannot simply be removed — it needs to be replaced. Not with wishful thinking, but with a counter-diagnosis built from your actual evidence. This is not positive thinking. It is accurate thinking. The label was wrong. What does the accurate picture of this quality in you actually look like?

The Evidence That Directly Contradicts the Label — In My Own History
The Accurate Statement — What the Evidence Actually Says About This Quality in Me
The Accurate Identity Statement — Present Tense, Evidence-Based
The Distinction That Matters

This is not an affirmation exercise. It is a correction exercise. You are not asserting something you hope will become true. You are correcting a verdict that the evidence already contradicts. The prescription was written for someone else. The accurate diagnosis — the one built from your own results, your own history, your own demonstrated capacity — was always available. You are simply reading it now, with the same precision you brought to the wrong one.

6
The Pressure Test
When the wrong label returns — and what to do when it does.

The wrong diagnosis will not disappear after one session. It will return — most reliably at the moments of highest stakes, in the rooms that matter most, at the thresholds of exactly the level it has been preventing you from holding. These scenarios prepare you for those moments before they arrive.

🎯 Scenario One — The Authority Figure Returns

Someone with apparent authority — a peer, a client, a collaborator, a social context — delivers feedback or a reaction that seems to confirm the wrong label. The system is confident again. What is the specific question you will ask yourself before you accept the verdict?

Not a motivational phrase — a precise question. The one that makes you look at the source, the basis, and the authority before you file the result.
🎯 Scenario Two — The Impostor Moment

You are in a room, or on a stage, or in a conversation that belongs to the level above your label — and the wrong diagnosis activates. "I don't belong here." "They are about to find out." The monitoring programme starts again. What is your pre-decided response — the one specific action you take immediately when this happens?

The action needs to be specific and physical — something that grounds you in the evidence of your counter-diagnosis rather than the anxiety of the label.
🎯 Scenario Three — The Result That Seems to Confirm the Label

Something does not go as planned. A result that feels like evidence for the wrong diagnosis. The deal falls through. The relationship ends. The pitch is not accepted. The label says: I told you so. What is the single reframe question that makes you examine the specific circumstance rather than generalise it into a verdict?

The wrong label generalises specific events into universal truths. The right question keeps the specific event specific — and keeps the counter-diagnosis intact.

Part Four

Return

The label is named. The origin is traced. The authority has been audited and found wanting. The counter-diagnosis is written. The cost of the wrong prescription is visible. Part Four is where the prescription is returned — through commitment, specific action, and a review structure that protects the work over time.

The Gap Action — This Week
The specific action that belongs to the person who has returned the wrong prescription.

Returning the prescription is not a feeling. It is not a decision made in the abstract. It is an action — a specific, dateable action that belongs to the version of you who looked at the result, saw your name on it, and said: this is not mine. Identify that action now, before you close this session.

The Gap Action — The Specific Thing the Person Who Has Returned This Label Would Do This Week Without Hesitation
The Day and Time I Will Take This Action
What I Will Do If the Wrong Label Activates Before I Take the Action
The Non-Negotiable

The gap action is not the end of the work. It is the beginning of the evidence base for the counter-diagnosis. Each action taken from above the label produces data that the nervous system uses to update the blueprint. The action does not need to be large. It needs to belong unmistakably to the person who has refused the wrong prescription. Take it before you feel ready. The readiness follows.

9
The Review Schedule
Protecting the work — and building in the checkpoints before they are needed.

The wrong diagnosis will reassert itself most forcefully at the moments of highest stakes and lowest support. Build the review schedule now — before those moments arrive. Put every date in your calendar before you close this document.

When What to Do Done
This Week Take the gap action named in Exercise 8. On the specific day, at the specific time. Read your counter-diagnosis the morning before you take it. The label will activate. Take the action anyway.
Within 7 Days Share the label you named and the gap action you took with one person — your accountability person. Use the exact language from this worksheet. Say it out loud. The naming externally is part of returning the prescription.
Day 14 Read the Counter-Diagnosis and Accurate Identity Statement from Exercise 5 every morning for 14 days. On Day 14, return to Exercise 2. Has the label's power shifted? What evidence has accumulated since the session to support the counter-diagnosis?
Day 30 Return to Exercise 7 — the Prescription Cost. Look at the financial, life, and identity costs. Has anything in those columns changed? What gap actions have been taken? What became possible that was previously blocked by the label?
Day 90 Run the full Label Inventory from Exercise 1 again. The label you worked on may have shifted — and revealed the next one beneath it. The work is iterative. The wrong diagnoses come in layers. Return to Part One and go deeper.
When the Label Returns Apply the question: are you sure this is for me? Then return to the Authority Audit in Exercise 4. Then read the counter-diagnosis. Then take the smallest possible action toward — not away from — the level the label is trying to prevent.
Commitment Contract

I Return the Prescription

Complete every field with the same precision you brought to the audit. Read it back aloud when you are done. This is the formal end of the monitoring programme.

Before You Close This

You have done what most people do not do. You have looked directly at a label that has been running your life — named it, traced it to its origin, audited the authority behind it, built the counter-diagnosis from your own evidence, calculated the cost, and committed to the action that returns the prescription.

The monitoring programme ends here. Not because the system changed. Because you named the error with sufficient precision that it has no answer.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know about yourself, or about the label, that you did not know when you opened this document. Not the most useful insight. The thing that cannot be unfelt.

The result had your name on it. The diagnosis was wrong. You never took this test. You were never in the room when this verdict was generated. And you were always allowed to ask the one question that ends it.

Are you sure this is for me?
The answer is no. Go live in full expression.
