Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is not a reflection exercise to be skimmed. It is the active coaching session that the episode prepared you for. Four parts. Nine exercises. One commitment. Designed to be completed in a single sitting — with full attention, without interruption, and with the same quality of honesty my wife brought to a rainy afternoon when she had every reason to stay inside.

The parts move in sequence. Do not skip ahead. The practical moves in Part Three will only carry full weight if you have done the honest excavation of Parts One and Two. The filter cannot be removed until it has been named — and it cannot be named until you have looked at it directly.

Write what is actually true. Not what sounds like the right answer. Not what you would say in a coaching session with someone watching. The honest answer is the only one that does the work.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the precise filter you have been running on your generosity, and the language it uses every time a giving moment arrives
  • Something traced — the origin experience that first taught you that giving came with a cost, a risk, or a vulnerability
  • Something separated — a clear distinction between the inherited rule protecting you and your actual capacity and desire to give
  • Something committed — the one specific act of unconditional giving you will complete before this week ends
Part One

Discover — The Filter You Have Built Around Your Giving

Before you can remove the giving filter, you need to see it clearly — how it operates, what it sounds like, and the specific moment it fires in your daily life. Part One builds that picture honestly.

1
The Last Giving Moment — Named Precisely
What you actually did the last time you had the opportunity to give without a guaranteed return.

Think about the last time someone in your world needed something you could have given. Not a large gesture. A small one — your time, your expertise, a word, a connection, an act. Someone who needed something you had. Not a stranger on another continent. Someone in your daily orbit.

📦 The Story — Hold This

She noticed a delivery in the postbox that was not hers. It was raining. The person who needed it lived on the other side of the building. There was no obligation, no relationship, no visible upside. She had every reason — practical, reasonable, defensible — to leave it where it was.

She picked it up and walked across in the rain. At the exact spot where she chose to give, life handed her what she had been waiting weeks to receive. The delivery driver who could not find our address was standing at the door of the building she had walked to for someone else.

The neighbour who watched the whole thing said: "You didn't have to go. But you did. And look at where you got your own package — right at the very spot where you went to deliver someone else's."

Coaching Prompt

Think about the last time a giving opportunity arrived in your life. Not what you would do in theory. What you actually did. And if you pulled back — if you calculated, hesitated, or decided today was not the day — what was the reason you gave yourself?

Not what sounds reasonable now. The actual reason, as it appeared in the moment. Write that first.

Notice ThisThe reason you just wrote is not the truth about your generosity. It is the language of the filter. The filter always has a reason. The work of this session is learning to recognise the filter's voice — because the filter always sounds reasonable.

2
The Filter Audit — Finding the Pattern
The reasons your giving filter has been using — and how often they have fired without you noticing.

The giving filter does not fire once. It fires every day, in small and large moments, often so quickly you do not notice it as a decision at all. This exercise slows it down and makes the pattern visible.

📮 The Postbox Principle — Hold This

Think about a physical postbox at the end of a street. If you stand at that postbox and refuse to put anything in it — because you are not sure the letter will arrive, because the person might not write back, because you are calculating whether the stamp is worth the investment — you will also never receive anything from it.

Not because the postbox is punishing you. But because the act of giving is what keeps you in relationship with the system. Generosity works exactly the same way. The filter that protects you from the cost of giving also removes you from the flow of life that returns things in forms you never could have planned.

Coaching Prompt

Think about the last week. How many times did a giving moment arrive — and the filter fired before you had consciously decided anything? A colleague who looked stuck. A neighbour who needed something. A person in your network you thought of reaching out to. A moment where you had something to give and the filter said "not now" or "not today" or "not my place."

This is not about guilt. This is data. Name what you find.

The Evidence of Return — What Giving Has Already Produced
The proof already in your own history that generosity without agenda has returned something real to you.

Before we trace where the filter came from, we need to catalogue the counter-evidence — the moments in your own life where giving without calculation produced something you did not expect. Not as theory. As documented fact from your own history.

Coaching Prompt

Think about a time you gave without expecting a return — and something came back. Not from the person you gave to. Something that arrived in a form you did not plan for. A connection. An opportunity. A relationship. A door that opened at the exact place where you had stopped to help someone else.

This evidence already exists. Most of us simply have not catalogued it. We do not count the returns on generosity the way we count the costs of it. Name what you find — even one example is enough to begin with.

Part Two

Define — Tracing the Filter to Where It Actually Came From

The giving filter was not a conscious decision. It was built from experience — a moment, an environment, or a season in your life where generosity cost you something significant. Part Two traces that origin. Not to assign blame. To understand why the filter was built — so you can decide whether it still serves you.

4
The Origin — When Giving First Felt Dangerous
Tracing the moment the filter was built — and the wound it has been protecting ever since.

Almost no one decides consciously to stop being generous. What happens instead is subtler. You give and get burned. You extend yourself and are taken advantage of. You help and it is used against you. You watch someone generous get exploited and decide quietly — without a formal announcement — that this is not going to be you. The filter builds gradually and then runs silently, long after the original situation has passed.

Coaching Prompt

When did you first learn that giving came with a cost? Not in those words. Not with a label. But when did you first absorb the lesson — through an experience, an environment, or a person — that leading with generosity put something at risk?

This may be a moment from years ago. It may be more recent. It may not be dramatic — sometimes the lessons that shape the filter most deeply are the quiet ones, the ones that accumulated rather than happened once. What comes up when you sit with that question honestly?

Notice ThisThe rule made sense when it was written. It was a rational response to a real experience. The question this session is asking is not whether the rule was justified then — it was. The question is whether it still accurately describes the world you are operating in now, and the people around you today.

5
The Gap Between the Rule and the Evidence
How well the filter's rule holds up against what you have actually observed.

The filter's rule was written about specific circumstances. But it has been applied across every circumstance since — regardless of how different those circumstances are from the original experience. This exercise checks how well the rule actually fits the situations you are applying it to today.

Coaching Prompt

Think about the people and situations where the filter is currently active — the ones where you are withholding something you could give. How similar are those people and situations to the original experience that wrote the rule?

Not "are there any similarities" — that question is always answered yes. But how comparable are they, really? What is the same, and what is genuinely different? Write both sides honestly.

Part Three

Pressure Test — When the Filter Fights Back

Removing the condition from your giving is not a single decision. The filter will resurface at specific trigger points — especially when you are tired, when the conditions are inconvenient, when you have been giving and not seen any return. Part Three prepares you for those moments.

6
Scenario One — When the Conditions Are All Wrong
When it is raining, when you are tired, when there is no visible upside.
⚡ Pressure Test Scenario

It is not a good day. You are stretched. A giving opportunity arrives — small but real. The conditions make it genuinely inconvenient. It will cost you something — time, energy, comfort, or ease. There is no visible economic return. There is no relationship obligation. There is no one watching. And the filter has a perfectly reasonable case for staying inside.

Coaching Prompt

My wife went out in the rain because a package had the wrong postbox and she knew the right one. The act of going was not extraordinary. The willingness to go — when the weather made a reasonable case for staying inside — was. What is your equivalent of that decision?

7
Scenario Two — When You Have Given and Seen No Return
When the filter is building its case from what has not come back.
⚡ Pressure Test Scenario

You have been generous. You have given your time, your expertise, your connections, your energy. And you have watched other people receive things that feel like they should have found their way to you. The return has not appeared in the form or the timeframe you expected. The filter is now building a case: the evidence is in — generosity does not pay off.

Coaching Prompt

The neighbour did not know — and my wife did not know — that the delivery driver was standing at that door. The return was not visible until she arrived. It could not have been planned for or predicted. What returns to you from generosity are almost never the ones you were looking for, in the timeframe you expected them, or from the person you gave to. What would change about how you interpret the current absence of return if you held that as true?

Not as a comfort. As a genuine shift in how you are reading the evidence.

Part Four

Protect — The Structure That Makes Generosity Durable

Removing the condition from your giving is not a single decision. It is a daily practice — a consistent return to the posture of giving before the filter has had time to build its case. Part Four builds the structure that makes that practice durable rather than temporary.

8
The 30-Day Giving Practice
The daily structure that presses the new posture down one act at a time.

The posture of generosity becomes durable through consistent, daily return — not through a single powerful insight. My wife did not decide once to be a generous person and then execute perfectly from that day forward. She lived it, repeatedly, in small moments, until it became the natural first response rather than a considered override of the filter.

  • Today — Before You Close This Worksheet: Complete the commitment contract below. Name the one act of unconditional giving you will take before this week ends, and write it down with a specific day and a specific person or situation. Do not defer this to tomorrow.
  • Every Morning for 30 Days: Before you check your phone, ask one question: "Who can I show kindness to today, and how?" Not a grand gesture — a specific, small, real act. Name it. This primes the giving posture before the day's filter opportunities arrive.
  • Every Evening for 30 Days: Name one act of giving from the day — however small. A word, an introduction, a moment of time given freely. The act of recognising what you gave each day trains the brain to see giving as a feature of your life rather than an exception to it.
  • Within 7 Days: Share the postbox story with one other person — not as a feel-good forward, but directly: "I heard this and it made me think about how I've been operating." External articulation makes the shift real in a way that private reflection alone does not.
  • Day 14: Return to Exercise 2 (The Filter Audit). Run it again on the previous two weeks. Has the filter fired less? Has the pattern changed? What new moments of giving have appeared that the filter would have prevented before this session?
  • Day 30: Return to Exercise 3 (The Evidence of Return). Add any new examples from the 30 days. Is the list longer? Is the pattern clearer? What has come back in the month since you removed the condition — in forms you did not expect?
9
The Gap Action — The First Unconditional Act
The specific giving act that belongs to the new posture — taken before the filter has time to produce a reason not to.

My wife did not think about whether to go. She just went. The first act of giving from the new posture is not something to schedule for a convenient future moment — it is the natural next step from this session. This exercise names it with enough specificity that it cannot be deferred.

Coaching Prompt

What is the one act of giving the filter has been preventing, deferring, or conditioning on circumstances that you know could go out this week — unconditionally, without calculating the return, regardless of the weather? The one that, if you did it with no agenda and no guarantee, would change something about how you show up in your world?

Not the category. The specific act. The specific person or situation. Named precisely enough that it cannot be turned into a vague intention.

Before You Close This

She went out in the rain. The package was not hers. The person who needed it lived on the other side of the building. There was no obligation, no relationship, no visible upside. She had every reason to leave it and every reason not to go.

And at the exact spot where she chose to give — in the rain, in the inconvenience, with no calculation of what it would produce — life handed her what she had been waiting weeks to receive. Not from the person she gave to. From a delivery driver who was about to give up, standing at the door of the very building she had walked to for someone else.

That is not magic. That is not luck. That is what happens when generosity stops being a conditional feature of your life and becomes the operating system. When you stop asking "what do I get?" and start asking "what can I give?" — not as a philosophy, but as a daily, inconvenient, rain-or-shine practice — life keeps putting you in the right place at the right time, in ways you never could have planned for.

The filter you identified in this session was built for a reason. It protected something real. But it has been running long past the circumstances that wrote it, in situations it was not designed for, costing you returns it could not let you see. You have named it now. You have traced it. You know what it has been protecting and where it has been over-applied.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know about your own generosity that you did not know when you opened it.

The filter can be released. The posture can be rebuilt. The giving that goes out unconditionally — in the rain, in the inconvenience, without a guaranteed return — finds its way back in forms that no calculation could have produced. My wife proved it with a package. Our neighbour named it on a rainy afternoon. Now it is your turn.

Choose the rain. Every day.
