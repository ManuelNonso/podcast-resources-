Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the coaching session the episode prepared you for — in written form, with the space to go further than a single listening allows. Four parts. Nine exercises. Three names, by the end, that you will have actually thanked, not just thought about.

The parts move in sequence. Do not skip ahead. The words you draft in Part Two only carry weight because of the honest excavation in Part One. And the commitment in Part Four only holds because you have already pressure tested it against the excuses that will try to talk you out of it in Part Three.

Write what is actually true. Not the tidy version. Not the version that makes you look thoughtful. The honest one — the names you have been avoiding, the reasons you have stayed quiet, the exact words you have never said out loud. That is what this session is for.

🧱 The Load-Bearing Wall — Hold This Throughout

Every building has two kinds of walls. Decorative walls get admired. They get the artwork, the accent colour, the compliments when someone walks into the room. Load-bearing walls get none of that. They are plain. Unremarkable. Rarely mentioned. But remove one, and the whole structure comes down.

Your life is built the same way. This worksheet is how you find your load-bearing walls, and finally point at them out loud.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the specific people who have been quietly holding your life together, and the exact ordinary thing they have done
  • Something traced — why you have stayed quiet this long, and where that belief about gratitude first came from
  • Something built — the exact words you will use, drafted in your own voice, ready to say out loud
  • Something committed — one specific call, to one specific person, within twenty-four hours of finishing this worksheet
Part One

Discover — Seeing Who Actually Holds You Up

Before you can thank the right people, you need to see them clearly — separated from the people who get the applause but were never actually load-bearing. Part One does that work.

1
The Wall Audit — Decorative vs Load-Bearing
Separating who gets the applause from who actually keeps your life standing.

This exercise begins with honesty about the current state of your relationships — not the flattering version. The goal is to see clearly enough that you stop confusing visibility with impact.

Coaching Prompt

If you had to remove every person who has quietly, consistently shown up for you, without ever asking for credit, what would collapse first in your life?

Not the people whose names come up in your acknowledgments or your social posts. The ones who never make it into the toast, but whose absence you would feel within a week. Where does that answer land in your chest?

2
The Origin — When Gratitude Started Needing an Occasion
Tracing the root belief that has kept you quiet.

Staying quiet is not a character flaw. It is usually an inherited rule, absorbed long before you had the words for it. You cannot dismantle a rule you have not traced.

Coaching Prompt

When did you first learn that saying "thank you, this mattered" out loud needed a special occasion to be appropriate? Not in those words. When did you first absorb the idea that expressing appreciation plainly, without a milestone attached, was unnecessary, or too much, or not your job to initiate?

Take yourself back to the earliest version of this, not the most recent. A household where feelings were assumed rather than spoken. A culture where gratitude was expected but rarely voiced. What comes up when you sit with that honestly?

Notice ThisThe rule that gratitude needs an occasion was written to avoid a moment of discomfort. It has been costing you and the people around you far more than that discomfort ever would.

3
The Room Test — Naming the Names, No Filter
If the room asked you to walk to someone right now, who would it be.

In Episode 50, a pastor asked a congregation to walk across the room and hold the hand of whoever had impacted them most, unexpectedly. Some of the most visibly successful people in that room were left standing untouched. This exercise puts you in that room.

Coaching Prompt

If you were asked, right now, to walk across a room and hold the hand of the person who has impacted you most, in a way you did not expect, who would you walk to?

Not the person you think you are supposed to name. The one your body actually moves toward. Name three. Be specific about the ordinary thing each of them did, not the vague, general praise.

Part Two

Define — Building Your Load-Bearing Wall List

Part Two turns the three names from Part One into a working list, and the exact words you will use to finally say what you have been meaning to say.

One page, three names, no ambiguity about what happens next.

5
The Words — Drafting Exactly What You'll Say
Writing it out first so the moment of the call does not talk you out of it.

You do not need a script when you call. But writing the words once, here, makes it far more likely you will actually say them plainly instead of softening them into something vague in the moment.

Coaching Prompt

If you had to say it in one breath, with no warm up and no hedging, what would you actually say to Person One?

Write it the way you would say it out loud, not the way it would look written in a card. Name the specific thing. That specificity is what makes it land.

Read it back out loud once before you close this worksheet. If it sounds like a card, rewrite it until it sounds like you.

Part Three

Pressure Test — Testing the List Against Real Life

A list on a page survives easily. A list under a busy week, an awkward reaction, or a public moment does not, unless you have already thought it through. Part Three does that thinking now, before the excuse arrives.

6
Scenario One — The Busy Week
What happens when life gets loud again before you've made the call.
The Scenario

You finish this worksheet with real intention. Then the week fills up. Deadlines, meetings, family demands. By day three, the thought has quietly slipped down the list, and by day seven you have convinced yourself you'll do it "next week, when things calm down."

7
Scenario Two — When They Deflect the Thanks
What to do when the other person waves it off.
The Scenario

You say it. And they respond the way genuinely humble, load-bearing people often do. "Oh, it was nothing." "I didn't really do anything." "You don't have to say that." The moment risks getting brushed past before it lands.

Coaching Prompt

If they deflect, what is the one specific sentence you can say next that holds the moment open instead of letting it close?

Something that names the specific thing again, rather than backing down from the gratitude. This is where most people fold. Decide now that you won't.

8
Scenario Three — Public Applause vs Private Acknowledgment
Choosing the right room for the right person.
The Scenario

In the church story from Episode 50, the wealthiest, most visibly successful people in the room got no hands held, and some grew visibly uncomfortable watching it happen. Public status does not always want public acknowledgment, and quiet consistency does not always need a stage either. Some thanks belong in a private phone call. Some belong to be said in front of others.

Part Four

Protect — Making This a Practice, Not a Moment

One phone call is a good start. It is not the whole shift. Part Four turns this worksheet into an ongoing practice, so the next load-bearing wall in your life does not go unnamed for years before you notice it.

9
The Ongoing Wall Check — Building the Habit
The simple, recurring practice that keeps you from waiting years again.

The goal is not to clear your list once. The goal is to build a habit of noticing and naming your load-bearing people before a decade passes and you find yourself making an overdue phone call again.

  • Within 24 Hours: Call Person One from your Load-Bearing Wall List. Say the words you drafted in Exercise 5. Not a text. A call, or a conversation in person.
  • Within 7 Days: Call Person Two. Use the same approach. Notice whether it gets easier or harder the second time, and why.
  • Within 14 Days: Call Person Three, or acknowledge them in the setting you identified in Exercise 8.
  • Every Month: Ask yourself one question. "Who has quietly shown up for me this month that I have not yet acknowledged?" Name one person. Tell them within the week.
  • Every Quarter: Revisit your Wall Audit from Exercise 1. Has anyone moved from decorative to load-bearing, or the reverse? Update the list honestly.

Before You Close This

My friend never thought of her prayer calls as impact. She thought of them as Tuesday. She loved God, she loved to pray, and she gathered people to do the thing she genuinely loved doing, expecting nothing back. It took a stranger holding her hand in a crowded room for her to hear what that ordinary faithfulness had actually meant to someone.

You do not have to wait for a pastor to ask a room to move, or for a stranger to walk toward you, to find out what your own load-bearing walls have meant to you. You already know their names. You named them in Part One.

The internet and the tools we now carry everywhere are extremely good at making us feel seen. A notification, a like, a comment, all of it mimics acknowledgment without ever requiring the courage of an actual conversation. Real acknowledgment still only happens one way. Someone has to say it, out loud, to someone else, on purpose.

Living in Full Expression is not only about what you build. It is about who you go back and thank on the way, before the moment convinces you it was never necessary in the first place.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know, that you did not know when you opened it.

The decorative walls will keep getting the applause. That was never going to change. But you now know exactly which walls in your life are load-bearing, exactly what they have done, and exactly what to say. The only thing left is the twenty-four hours.
