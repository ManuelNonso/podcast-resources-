Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the coaching session the episode prepared you for — in written form, with room to go further than a single listening allows. Four parts. Nine exercises. One inherited belief traced to its true origin, and declared paid off by the end of it.

The parts move in sequence. Part One is where you separate what you inherited from what you actually chose. Part Two turns that clarity into a declaration. Part Three pressure tests it against the moments most likely to pull the old belief back into place. Part Four makes sure it doesn't quietly transfer to whoever comes after you.

Write what is actually true, not the version that stays loyal to the people who passed this to you. Naming where a belief came from is not blame. It is the first honest step toward finally closing the account.

🧱 The Inherited Debt — Hold This Throughout

Some families inherit debt across generations, compounding with every hardship, until someone finally traces it, names it, and pays it off in full. Beliefs work the same way. They compound quietly across generations until someone stops the pattern on purpose.

You were born into a psychological framework you didn't build. This worksheet is how you find out exactly what you're still paying interest on, and what it will take to close the account for good.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the specific inherited belief currently running decisions you thought were entirely your own
  • Something traced — whose experience it actually belonged to, and the real circumstance that made it reasonable for them
  • Something separated — their era and danger from your actual reality now
  • Something committed — a spoken declaration and one visible action that stops the belief from transferring any further
Part One

Discover — Seeing the Debt Clearly

Before you can pay off an inherited belief, you need to see it plainly, know whose it actually was, and notice who might already be inheriting it from you.

1
The Blueprint Audit — Inherited vs Chosen
Separating what you built from what you were simply handed.

This exercise begins with an honest inventory of the beliefs currently running your decisions. Not the version that sounds like personality. The real one.

Coaching Prompt

What fear, belief, or instinct do you carry that you've never actually examined, the kind that simply feels like "who you are"?

Think about an automatic reaction around money, visibility, trust, or love. Not what you think the answer should be. What actually comes up when you're honest about it.

Notice ThisIf a belief in your first column, on closer look, has no real personal experience behind it, move it. Most people find their list is far more inherited than expected.

2
The Origin — Whose Loan Is This
Tracing the belief back to the person and circumstance that formed it.

Choose the one inherited belief from Exercise 1 with the strongest grip on your life right now. This exercise, and the rest of the worksheet, will focus on that one.

Coaching Prompt

Whose loan is this actually? When did you first absorb it, and what real circumstance made it a reasonable survival strategy for them, at that time, in that danger?

Trace it to the earliest source you can identify. Was it taught directly, or simply modelled and absorbed through the atmosphere of your home?

Notice ThisNaming this is not disloyalty to the person who passed it to you. It is simply seeing clearly, for the first time, that the debt was theirs before it became yours.

3
The Transference Check
Noticing who may already be inheriting this from you.

In Episode 54, several clients realised, once the inherited belief was named, that they were already passing the exact same thing to their own children. This exercise asks the same of you, with compassion, not guilt.

Coaching Prompt

Who have you already, unknowingly, started passing this exact belief to? A child, a mentee, someone watching you closely enough to absorb it the same way you once did.

Not through a lecture. Through the atmosphere you've been modelling. Where might they already be quietly picking this up?

"I felt like I'd been about to hand my daughter the exact same debt I'd spent my whole life quietly paying off, without her ever having asked for it."
Part Two

Define — Building Your Declaration

Part Two turns what you named in Part One into a clear declaration and one visible action that begins closing the account.

4
Declare It Paid — Your Working Builder
One page. The debt, the evidence it's outdated, and the new belief.

This is the working document everything else refers back to. Keep it specific and grounded in real evidence, not just hope.

Your Generational Reset

Name It, Evidence It, Replace It

5
The Words — Speaking the Declaration
Preparing the sentence you will say out loud.

A belief that has only ever been thought quietly is far easier to cancel out loud than in silence. This exercise drafts the exact words.

Coaching Prompt

If you had to cancel this debt in one clear sentence, spoken out loud, what would you actually say?

Write it the way you'd say it to yourself in a quiet, serious moment, not the way it would sound on a poster.

Read it out loud once before moving on. If it feels like a real decision rather than a wish, it's ready.

Part Three

Pressure Test — Testing the Declaration Against Real Life

A declaration survives easily in writing. It does not survive as easily under stress, family reaction, or a live moment with the very person you named in Exercise 3, unless you've already thought it through.

6
Scenario One — The Old Fear Under Stress
When pressure makes the inherited belief feel true again.
The Scenario

A stressful week arrives, money is tight, or visibility feels risky again, and the old inherited belief resurfaces with its full original force, as if the declaration from Exercise 5 never happened.

7
Scenario Two — Family Discomfort With Your Change
When breaking the pattern unsettles the person who passed it to you.
The Scenario

The relative or figure whose belief you traced in Exercise 2 notices you acting differently, and responds with discomfort, confusion, or subtle pressure to return to the old pattern, even without meaning any harm by it.

Coaching Prompt

How can you hold both compassion for where their belief came from, and firmness about not carrying it forward yourself?

This is not about confrontation. It's about staying steady in the new belief without needing their approval of it.

8
Scenario Three — Catching the Transfer Live
When you catch yourself about to model the old belief.
The Scenario

In a real, unplanned moment, with the person named in Exercise 3 watching, you catch yourself about to say or do the very thing that would pass the old belief forward, out of habit, before you've had time to think.

Part Four

Protect — Making This a Practice, Not a Moment

One declaration is a good start. It is not the whole reset. Part Four turns this worksheet into an ongoing audit, so the next inherited belief gets caught early, before it has four generations to compound.

9
The Ongoing Blueprint Check — Building the Habit
The simple, recurring practice that catches the next inherited debt early.

The goal is not to pay off one belief and stop. The goal is to build the habit of noticing quickly when an inherited pattern is running your decisions, or beginning to transfer to someone else.

  • Within 7 Days: Take the visible action named in Exercise 4, by the deadline you set, and speak the declaration from Exercise 5 out loud at least once.
  • The Day After: Write down what actually happened, and whether the old belief or the new one showed up stronger.
  • Every Month: Ask yourself one question. "What have I been modelling this month that I wouldn't want inherited?"
  • Every Quarter: Revisit your Blueprint Audit from Exercise 1. Has a new inherited belief surfaced? Name it while it's still fresh.
  • Whenever the Old Fear Resurfaces: Return to the mechanism from Exercise 6 before letting stress hand the debt back its original weight.

Before You Close This

Those families near the Pakistan border didn't choose the debt they were born into. For four generations, it simply grew, compounded by sickness and circumstance, until someone finally intervened and paid it off, in full, in a single act.

You don't need someone else to intervene on your behalf. You have already done the tracing, the naming, and the declaring. The debt ends with the decision you make this week, not with someone else's rescue.

Living in Full Expression means authoring your own life, not ghostwriting someone else's unfinished story, and making sure the people who come after you inherit something lighter than what you carried.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know, that you did not know when you opened it.

The old belief will still make its case under pressure, familiar and convincing. You now know exactly whose debt it was, exactly what your reality actually looks like now, and exactly what you'll say and do when it resurfaces. The only thing left is the seven days.

Go pay off what was never yours to carry.
