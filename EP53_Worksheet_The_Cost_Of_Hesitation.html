Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the coaching session the episode prepared you for — in written form, with room to go further than a single listening allows. Four parts. Nine exercises. One honest diagnosis, and one small step, taken by the end of it.

This worksheet is built differently from a straight line. From Part Two onward, it branches into two paths, fear and genuine uncertainty, because they are not the same problem. Read both branches before choosing. Many people find they are further down one path than they expected.

Write what is actually true, not the version that sounds more decisive. If you're genuinely unsure whether this is even the right thing, that is not a lesser answer than fear. It deserves just as much care, and a different next step.

🍇 Rotting on the Vine — Hold This Throughout

Fruit doesn't wait for perfect weather to ripen. It reaches its moment, and it must be picked, or it begins to rot right there on the branch, not because anything went wrong, but because time did not pause for the picking to feel convenient.

The best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago. The second best time is today. This worksheet is how you find out exactly which "someday" you've been letting rot, and what to do about it starting now.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the specific "someday" you've been carrying, and how long it's actually been sitting there
  • Something diagnosed — an honest distinction between fear-based hesitation and genuine, clarity-seeking uncertainty
  • Something separated — starting badly from failing permanently, and endless research from real evidence
  • Something committed — one small, real step or experiment, with a deadline, taken within the next seven days
Part One

Discover — Naming What's Been Decaying

Before you can act, you need to see clearly what you've been postponing, how long it's been sitting there, and which kind of hesitation is actually keeping it in place.

1
The Someday Audit
Naming the specific intention, and how long it's actually been decaying.

This exercise begins with an honest inventory. Not the version that makes the delay sound reasonable. The real one.

Coaching Prompt

What is the "someday" you have been carrying, the thing you've already decided, somewhere inside, that you want to do, but keep pushing to a future date that never actually arrives?

Name it specifically. Then be honest about how long it's actually been sitting there. Weeks. Months. Years. Where does that real number land in your chest?

The Someday, Named Specifically
How Long It's Actually Been Sitting There

Notice ThisIf you've told this "someday" to more than one person, more than once, without ever attaching a real timeline, that repetition is often a sign the fruit has already started to decay.

2
The Origin — Tracing Where the Hesitation Started
Finding the earliest root, whichever path it turns out to be.

Hesitation is not a character flaw. It was formed somewhere specific, whether it's fear or genuine uncertainty. You cannot address a root you have not traced.

Coaching Prompt

When you imagine finally starting, what is the very first feeling that shows up? Is it a specific dread about being seen or judged, or is it a genuine, honest not-knowing about whether this is even the right thing?

Sit with the actual feeling rather than the answer you think sounds better. Trace it back to the earliest time you remember feeling this exact way about a decision.

Notice ThisBoth roots are legitimate. Fear was formed to protect you from something painful. Uncertainty was formed to protect your limited time from being wasted. Neither one is weakness.

3
The Diagnosis — Fear or Genuine Uncertainty
Naming plainly which path you're actually on.

This is the single most important exercise in this worksheet. Getting the diagnosis right changes everything that follows. Don't rush it.

Coaching Prompt

Ask yourself honestly: has waiting actually given you more clarity, or has it simply given you more time to spin the same questions without any new information?

If waiting has produced no new information, this is very likely fear, dressed as prudence. If you genuinely cannot tell whether this is the right pursuit, even with real reflection, this is likely honest uncertainty.

"Starting badly is not the same as failing permanently. It is one data point in a much longer story, not a verdict on who you are."

5
The Words — Drafting What You'll Tell Yourself
Preparing the sentence for the moment doubt shows back up.

Doubt will very likely return the moment you take this step. Writing the response now, while you're clear-headed, makes it far more available in the moment you actually need it.

Coaching Prompt

If your diagnosis is fear, what is the one sentence that separates starting badly from failing permanently, in your own words? If your diagnosis is uncertainty, what is the one sentence that reminds you real evidence beats more thinking?

Write it the way you would actually say it to yourself, not the way it would sound on a poster.

Read it back out loud once before moving on. If it sounds like something you'd tell a friend you love, it's ready.

Part Three

Pressure Test — Testing the Plan Against Real Life

A plan survives easily in writing. It does not survive as easily under comparison, an unclear result, or a discouraging first attempt, unless you've already thought it through. Part Three does that thinking now.

6
Scenario One — Someone Else's Highlight Reel
When comparison makes you feel like it's already too late.
The Scenario

You see someone your age, or younger, celebrated for mastery in something close to your own "someday." The instinct is to feel behind, as if the window has already closed, when really you're only seeing the finished version of years you haven't lived yet.

7
Scenario Two — The Experiment Doesn't Give a Clear Answer
When the uncertainty path leaves you still unsure.
The Scenario

You run the smallest experiment from Exercise 4, and the result is genuinely mixed. Not a clear yes, not a clear no. It would be easy to conclude the experiment failed, or that you're back to square one.

8
Scenario Three — The Discouraging First Attempt
When the fear path's first step confirms the fear instead of easing it.
The Scenario

You take the imperfect step from Exercise 4, and it goes badly. The rejection, the awkward attempt, the quiet embarrassment, all of it feels like proof the original fear was right all along.

Part Four

Protect — Making This a Practice, Not a Moment

One small step is a good start. It is not the whole shift. Part Four turns this worksheet into an ongoing practice, so the next "someday" does not get to sit and decay for years before you notice it.

9
The Weekly Fruit Check — Building the Habit
The simple, recurring practice that catches decay early next time.

The goal is not to make one decision well. The goal is to build the habit of noticing quickly when a "someday" has started to sit too long.

  • Within 7 Days: Take the step or run the experiment named in Exercise 4, by the deadline you set, whether or not full confidence has arrived.
  • The Day After: Write down what actually happened, and compare it honestly to what the fear or the uncertainty predicted.
  • Every Week: Ask yourself one question. "What have I been calling 'someday' this week that's actually ready to become 'today'?"
  • Every Month: Revisit your Someday Audit from Exercise 1. Has anything new started decaying? Name it while it's still fresh.
  • Whenever Comparison Hits: Run the reminder from Exercise 6 before letting someone else's timeline convince you yours has closed.

Before You Close This

The person I once saw celebrated on screen had thirteen years behind them, not thirteen years of certainty, thirteen years of starting, adjusting, and continuing. The mastery came after the starting, not before it.

Whichever path brought you here, fear or genuine uncertainty, the answer was never going to arrive by waiting a little longer. It arrives by picking the fruit that's ready now, even without knowing exactly how the rest of the orchard will turn out.

Living in Full Expression means telling the difference between fear and uncertainty with honesty, and refusing to let either one keep your potential decaying in silence for one more season.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know, that you did not know when you opened it.

The fruit will keep ripening whether you pick it or not. You now know exactly which someday has been decaying, exactly which kind of hesitation it is, and exactly what your first honest step looks like. The only thing left is the seven days.

Pick the fruit before it rots.
Live in full expression.
