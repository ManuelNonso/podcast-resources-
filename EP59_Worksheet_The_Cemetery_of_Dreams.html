Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the Deep Edition. Every exercise here goes further than a single sitting usually allows, and Exercise 2 has been rebuilt entirely as a full root-cause and childhood tracing process, because most waiting was never really about the thing you're waiting on. It was learned, somewhere, a long time before you knew you were learning it.

This worksheet is not about an old man on a road. It is about what you have been quietly filing under "someday," in your relationships, your dreams, what you give to others, and your own health and growth. Find the one costing you the most sleep, and work that one all the way through.

Give yourself real time with this, ideally in more than one sitting. The root-cause section in particular is not meant to be rushed. If a memory surfaces that feels bigger than this worksheet can hold, that is worth bringing to a therapist or counselor, not just a page.

From Part Two onward, this worksheet branches into two paths, things that involve another person's clock as well as your own, and things that depend only on your own time and energy, because they carry different urgency and call for different first moves. Read both branches before choosing.

🕯️ The Candle — Hold This Throughout

Some candles burn brightly right up until their very last moment, giving off their fullest light for as long as they are lit. Others sit for years, wax fully intact, wick untouched, technically still a candle, but never having done the one thing a candle exists to do.

The tragedy is never that a candle eventually runs out. Every candle does. The tragedy is a candle that never got to burn at all. This worksheet exists to make sure yours does, and to understand exactly who or what first told you to keep the flame low.

What This Deep Edition Will Produce
  • Something named — the specific dream, relationship, or act of giving you have been filing under "someday," across every major area of your life, and its true cost so far
  • Something traced — not just the moment waiting began, but the family messaging, the modeled behavior, and the exact inherited belief still quietly running the decision
  • Something separated — genuine unreadiness, from a fear of not having time to recover, from a belief that was never actually yours to begin with
  • Something committed — one dated, visible first step, taken within the next seven days, built on a belief you consciously chose rather than one you simply inherited
Part One

Discover — Naming It, and Tracing It All the Way Back

Before you can act, you need to see clearly what you're actually waiting on, what it has already cost you, and where the belief that it was safer to wait was first planted in you, sometimes decades before this moment.

1
The Someday List
An honest inventory across every area where "someday" tends to hide, and what it has already cost.

Most people have never written this list out fully, in one place, at one time. That is exactly why it stays vague and stays postponed.

Coaching Prompt

Be specific. Not "spend more time with family," but the actual conversation, visit, or apology you've been putting off. The cost question matters as much as the naming.

2
The Root — A Full Tracing of Where the Waiting Began
A deeper, four part root-cause and childhood tracing process. Take your time here.

Look back at your four answers in Exercise 1. Choose the one you will work through for the rest of this worksheet, the one that would change the most about how you feel at the end of each day.

2a — The Family Script
Coaching Prompt

What did the adults around you, growing up, model or say, directly or indirectly, about risk, dreams, failure, and waiting for "the right time"?

Think about what was actually done in your household, not just what was said. Children absorb behavior more than advice.

2b — The Formative Memory
Coaching Prompt

When did you first learn that this particular kind of waiting felt safer than acting? Was there a specific moment, a specific failure, or a specific piece of feedback that planted it?

Take your time. For some, this traces back to a single sharp memory. For others, it's a slow accumulation with no one clear starting point, and that's equally valid to name.

2c — The Inherited Belief
Coaching Prompt

If the family script and the formative memory were compressed into a single sentence, a rule you've been unconsciously obeying ever since, what would that sentence be?

Write it exactly as it would sound if said out loud, in the voice it was likely first said in. It might be a sentence someone actually said to you, or one you quietly wrote for yourself based on what you observed.

2d — The Cost Tally
Coaching Prompt

Now count the real cost. Not in a punishing way, but in an honest one. What has obeying this inherited belief already cost you, across the years you've carried it?

Include opportunities, relationships, confidence, or simply years of peace you didn't get to have.

Notice ThisThe belief you just wrote down was very likely a reasonable conclusion once, made by a version of you with far less power, information, or safety than you have now. Naming it precisely is not blame, on you or on whoever modeled it. It is the first honest step toward no longer obeying a rule that was written for a much younger version of your life.

3
The Diagnosis — Whose Clock Is Running
Naming plainly whether this depends only on your time, or also on someone else's.

This diagnosis determines which path you'll build in Part Two, and it matters more than it might seem at first.

Coaching Prompt

Does the thing you named in Exercise 2 involve another person whose own time is also limited, a parent, a friend, someone you've been meaning to reach, or is it something that depends only on your own energy and years, a dream, a skill, a piece of work?

Both carry real urgency. They are just not the same urgency, and they don't call for the same first move.

"Someday is not a date. It is the place where dreams go to die quietly, while life keeps moving forward."

Part Three

Pressure Test — Testing the Plan Against Real Life

A plan survives easily in writing. It does not survive as easily against a poor response, a busy month, or a fresh wave of fear, unless you've already thought it through. Part Three does that thinking now.

6
Scenario One — It Doesn't Go the Way You Hoped
When the reach out goes unanswered, or the first rep falls flat.
The Scenario

You take the first reach out or the first rep from Exercise 4, and it doesn't go the way you hoped. The message goes unanswered. The first attempt at the dream falls flat. It would be easy to conclude the whole thing was a mistake, and that waiting had been the wiser choice all along.

This scenario tests whether your new plan can survive a bad first result, not just a good one.
7
Scenario Two — A Month Passes and Nothing Moved
When life gets busy and the deadline quietly slides.
The Scenario

Life gets genuinely busy, and a month passes without the reach out or the first rep actually happening. The old habit of waiting starts to feel familiar and almost comfortable again.

This scenario tests whether the plan survives ordinary life, not just a clear, motivated week.
8
Scenario Three — Small Progress Brings Back Bigger Fear
When early success makes the stakes feel higher, not lower.
The Scenario

The first reach out or first rep actually goes well. Instead of relief, a new fear shows up, one about what happens if you keep going and the bigger attempt doesn't go as well as the first small one did.

This scenario tests whether the plan survives success, which unsettles the inherited belief even more than failure does.
Part Four

Protect — Making This a Practice, Not a Moment

One reach out or one first rep is a good start. It is not the whole shift. Part Four turns this worksheet into an ongoing practice, so the next "someday" doesn't get to run quietly for years before you notice it.

9
The Weekly Practice — Building the Habit
The simple, recurring practice that catches "someday" early next time, plus a relapse plan.

The goal is not to close one someday. The goal is to build the habit of noticing quickly when something has quietly moved from "soon" to "someday," and to already know what to do if the old pattern returns in full.

  • Within 7 Days: Take the first reach out or first rep named in Exercise 4, by the deadline you set.
  • The Day After: Write down what actually happened, and how it compares to what the fear predicted.
  • Every Week: Ask yourself one question. "What have I been calling 'someday' this week that I could give a real date instead?"
  • Every Month: Revisit your Someday List from Exercise 1, and your inherited belief from Exercise 2c. Has a new one started forming? Name it while it's still fresh.
  • Whenever You Think of Someone Who's Aged Visibly: Let that be the reminder to check your own someday list, not just theirs.

Before You Close This

The old man crossing that road was once exactly as young, as energetic, and as full of dreams as any of us. Somewhere along the way, the energy left his body, but the story never stopped being written. Someday, in one way or another, all of us become him, looking back on the life we actually lived, and on the beliefs we let quietly run it.

The wealthiest place on earth is not a gold mine or an oil field, it is the place where untried dreams and unsaid words are quietly buried. Whatever you named in this worksheet, it does not have to end up there. The candle has not gone out. It just hasn't been lit yet, and now you know exactly whose hand has been keeping the flame low.

Living in Full Expression means giving what you have, however small, today, guided by a belief you chose rather than one you simply inherited.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know, that you did not know when you opened it.

Someday will still not be a real date tomorrow. You now know exactly what you've been waiting on, exactly where that waiting was first learned, and exactly what your first honest move looks like. The only thing left is the seven days.

Live it now, before it becomes a regret.
