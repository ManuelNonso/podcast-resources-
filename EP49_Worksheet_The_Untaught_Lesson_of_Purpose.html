Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the coaching session the episode prepared you for — in written form, with the space to go further than a single listening allows. Four parts. Ten exercises. One purpose statement at the end that belongs entirely to you, that no algorithm can write, and that no automation can make irrelevant.

The parts move in sequence. Do not skip ahead. The purpose statement in Part Three has no weight if you have not done the honest excavation of Parts One and Two. The protection protocol in Part Four only works if what it is protecting is real — and the realness comes from the work that precedes it.

Write what is actually true. The AI-optimised world has enough polished outputs. This worksheet is asking for something rarer: the honest account of who you actually are, what you have actually experienced, and what is actually present in you that no training run has ever been able to reproduce. That is what this session is for.

🔍 The X-Ray — Hold This Throughout

AI is like an X-ray machine. It does not replace your skeleton. It makes your skeleton visible. This worksheet is the X-ray. It will show what is actually there — the genuine depth, the real dimensions, the specific human presence that your professional life has been built on, knowingly or not. Some of what the X-ray shows will be solid. Some of it will point to gaps. Both are useful. Both are data. The only response that is not useful is looking away.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the precise dimensions of human depth that are most genuinely yours, in language specific enough to act on
  • Something traced — the origin of the identity built on output, and how far back the conflation of your worth with your utility actually goes
  • Something built — a personal purpose statement that is automation-proof, grounded in the five dimensions, and written in your own voice
  • Something committed — a 30-day practice and a specific first action that begins the shift from output-based identity to depth-based identity, this week
Part One

Discover — Seeing Your Identity Clearly

Before you can build an identity that automation cannot touch, you need to see the identity you currently have — honestly, precisely, and without the polished version standing in the way. Part One does that work.

1
The Utility Audit — What You Are Currently Being Paid For
Mapping the honest split between what you produce and who you are.

This exercise begins with honesty about the current state — not the aspirational state. The goal is not to feel good about what you find. The goal is to see it clearly enough to make a deliberate choice about what to build on.

Coaching Prompt

If your employer, your clients, or your community had access to everything AI can now do — at no cost, with no friction, available immediately — what would they still need you for?

Not what you hope they would need you for. Not the job description. The honest answer. Where does that answer feel solid — and where does it feel uncertain? Both matter.

Because you cannot change what you cannot name.

2
The Origin — When You First Tied Your Worth to Your Output
Tracing the root of the productivity trap in your own formation.

The productivity trap is not primarily a technology problem. It is a formation problem. Most of us were taught — gradually, without a single deliberate lesson — that our value as a person is a function of what we produce. AI did not create this. It revealed it. And you cannot dismantle what you have not traced.

Coaching Prompt

When did you first learn that your worth was measured by your output? Not in those words — but when did you first absorb the lesson that producing more, performing better, achieving higher made you more valuable as a person?

Take yourself back. Not to the most recent version of this experience. To the earliest one you can locate. The school environment that praised results over curiosity. The household that celebrated achievement over presence. The workplace that rewarded delivery over development. What comes up when you sit with that question honestly?

The Rule You Built From It

Notice ThisThe rule that your worth is your output was written about a world where the output was exclusively yours. That world has ended. The rule has not updated. This exercise is the beginning of the update.

3
The Alive Moments — When the Depth Was Already Working
Finding the evidence that your irreplaceable value already exists and has always been present.

Before we name and build the five dimensions, we need to find them where they have already appeared in your professional and personal life. They are already there. They have been showing up for years. Most people simply have not catalogued them — because the output got the credit and the depth went unnamed.

Coaching Prompt

Think about the moments in your professional life when you felt most genuinely alive, most useful, most connected to why you do what you do. Not the moments when you delivered the most. The moments when something real happened — when a conversation shifted something, when a decision required your specific way of seeing, when another person felt genuinely seen by you in a way that changed something for them.

These moments are not accidents. They are pointing to the dimensions of human depth that are most alive in you. Name three. Be specific — not "I helped someone" but what specifically happened, what you brought to it that no one else in the room could have brought in quite the same way.

Part Two

Define — Mapping Your Five Dimensions

The five dimensions of irreplaceable human depth are not abstract virtues. They are specific, observable, cultivatable capacities. Part Two maps them honestly against your own life — showing you which ones are already strong, which need development, and which one is the axis your purpose statement will be built on.

4
The Five Dimensions — Your Honest Scorecard
Rating your current strength in each dimension — and naming the evidence for the rating you give.

For each of the five dimensions, read the definition carefully. Then rate your current strength honestly — not aspirationally. Three filled dots means this dimension is genuinely alive and demonstrable in your professional life right now. One filled dot means it is present but underdeveloped. Use the evidence field beneath each row to name one specific example that justifies your rating.

Before You Score

The question for each dimension is not "do I value this?" Almost everyone values all five. The question is: can I name a specific, recent, observable instance of this dimension operating in my professional life — one that another person could confirm if asked?

If yes, the dimension is genuinely present. If you can only describe it aspirationally — "I would like to be better at this" — it is a development area, not a current strength. Be honest. The scorecard is only useful if it reflects what is actually there.

1. Contextual Judgment
Reading what is not being said. Making calls based on the full situation, not just the data. Sensing the emotional undercurrent of a room.
2. Genuine Human Connection
Creating real, felt, mutual connection — making another person feel genuinely seen, heard, and accompanied. Not rapport. Connection.
3. Creative Intuition
Originating — bringing into being what has never existed before, from the singular combination of your experience, perspective, and failures.
4. Moral Presence
Who you are when no one is watching. The consistency of your character under pressure. Being the same person in private as in public.
5. The Capacity to Be With
Sitting with another person in difficulty without rushing them toward resolution. Pure, non-directive, non-anxious presence in hard moments.
5
The Deepest Dimension — Your Primary Human Anchor
Identifying the single dimension that is most genuinely, irreplaceably yours.

Of the five dimensions, one of them is your primary anchor — the one where you are most alive, most genuinely yourself, most irreplaceable. It is not necessarily the one you are most recognised for. It is the one that, when you are operating from it fully, feels most like you.

Coaching Prompt

If you removed every output from your professional life — every report, every deliverable, every measurable result — and asked the people who have worked most closely with you what they would most miss, what would they say? Not what they would say if prompted. What would come to them first, unprompted, when they thought about what your presence had actually given them?

That answer — whatever it is — is pointing directly at your primary dimension. It may or may not match what you scored highest above. Go with what the people who have experienced you would say.

It is what makes you an irreplaceable human being in the specific context of your work. Those are different things — and in the age of AI, the difference matters more than it ever has.

6
The Gap — Where Depth Has Been Substituted by Output
The honest accounting of where the X-ray shows something needs building.

The X-ray exercise is only complete if you look at what needs building as honestly as you looked at what is already strong. This exercise maps the gap — the specific areas where technical output has been functioning as a substitute for genuine human depth, and where development is needed rather than just recognition.

📖 The Workshop — Two Voices, One Lesson

One participant saw the danger: AI producing polished outputs that mask the gap between what a person claims to be and what they can actually deliver. He was right. The gap is real, and it is dangerous because it is invisible until the moment it is not.

The other participant — younger, on the other side of the room — was thriving. AI handled the mechanical production. Her time, energy, and presence went to what the algorithm cannot do: the client conversations, the creative problems, the relationships, the judgment calls. She was not using AI to cover a gap. She was using it to free up space for the depth that was genuinely there.

This exercise asks you to name, honestly, which of those two pictures more closely resembles your current reality — and in what specific areas.

Coaching Prompt

Here is the reframe that changes the relationship with what you just found: the gap you named is not a verdict about your worth. It is a map of your next investment. The person who can see the gap and name it honestly is already ahead of the person who cannot. What would it mean to treat this gap not as evidence of inadequacy but as the most important professional development information you have received this year?

Part Three

Build — Your Automation-Proof Purpose Statement

Part Three is the construction phase. Everything in Parts One and Two has been building toward this: a personal purpose statement grounded in your deepest dimension, your genuine human depth, and the specific contribution you are here to make that no algorithm can replicate or replace.

7
The AI Amplifier Plan — Using Technology to Free the Depth
Redesigning your relationship with AI so it serves your depth rather than substituting for it.

The person thriving in the AI era is not the person who avoids the technology. It is the person who has configured the technology to do the mechanical work so that their time and presence can go to the human dimensions that actually define their value. This exercise builds that configuration for your specific role and depth.

Coaching Prompt

Think about your current working week. Where does your time and energy go? For each major time investment, ask: is this the kind of work that builds or expresses genuine human depth — or is it production work that a capable model could do adequately?

The goal is not to automate everything that can be automated. The goal is to free up specific time and energy for the specific human dimensions where your irreplaceable value lives. Where would you put that freed time? What would you do with two more hours per week of depth-level work?

8
The Human Connection Inventory — Where Real Connection Lives in Your Work
Mapping the specific relationships and interactions where genuine human connection is most present and most needed.

Genuine human connection is not a soft skill. It is, in an increasingly automated world, the rarest and most economically valuable capacity a professional can possess. This exercise maps where real connection — not rapport, not networking, not managed relationships, but actual genuine human connection — exists or could exist in your work.

Coaching Prompt

Think about the people in your professional life — clients, colleagues, collaborators, the people you lead or are led by. For which of them do you have a relationship that could honestly be described as genuine? Where another person feels truly seen by you — not just managed, not just serviced, but actually seen and accompanied?

Now think about the people in your professional life where the relationship is functional but not genuine — where you show up adequately but not fully. Not to judge those relationships, but to notice them. Because those are the relationships where your depth is not currently reaching — and where it could.

9
The Purpose Statement — Built from the Depth Up
Writing the automation-proof statement of why you are here and what you specifically offer that no algorithm can replicate.

This is the exercise the whole worksheet has been building toward. A personal purpose statement that is not a mission statement, not a brand tagline, and not an aspiration. It is a precise, honest, grounded description of the specific human depth you bring, the specific people it serves, and the specific difference it makes that could not be made without you.

Your Purpose Statement Builder

Build It in Four Parts

Your purpose statement will emerge from combining these four parts in your own voice. It does not need to be elegant. It needs to be true. Draft it in the field below — using the four parts as your raw material, but writing it as a single, flowing statement in your own words.

Coaching Prompt — After You Have Written It

Read your purpose statement back aloud. Not in your head. Aloud. Does it sound like you — the real you, not the polished professional version? Does it name something specific enough that a person who knows you would immediately recognise it? Does it make you feel something — a quiet rightness, a sense of being seen by your own words?

If yes — this is the statement. If something feels off — go back and go deeper. The statement is only finished when it is genuinely true, not just well-written.

Part Four

Protect — The Practice That Makes the Depth Durable

Shifting from an output-based identity to a depth-based identity is not a single decision. It is a daily practice — a consistent return to the dimensions that make you irreplaceable, deliberately cultivated until they become the natural operating system rather than the occasional override. Part Four builds that practice.

10
The 30-Day Depth Practice — Building the New Operating System
The daily and weekly structure that presses the depth-based identity into place one deliberate act at a time.

The shift from output-based to depth-based identity is built the same way every identity shift is built — through the accumulated evidence of deliberate choices. Thirty days of daily, specific, intentional practice does not complete the shift. It begins it in a way that no single insight or resolution can.

  • Today — Before You Close This Worksheet: Complete the commitment contract on the next page. Name your purpose statement and your first amplifier action. Do not defer either to tomorrow.
  • Every Morning for 30 Days: Before you open your email, read your purpose statement aloud. One sentence. The whole thing. This is not a motivational ritual — it is a cognitive anchor. It sets the frame before the day's output demands arrive.
  • Every Evening for 30 Days: Ask one question: "Where did my depth show up today?" Not "what did I produce?" Where did contextual judgment, genuine connection, creative intuition, moral presence, or the capacity to be with actually operate in my work today? Name it. One instance. This trains the identity to locate value in depth rather than output.
  • Within 7 Days: Have one depth-led conversation — a conversation whose sole purpose is genuine human connection, with no deliverable attached. No agenda. No outcome to achieve. Just the practice of being fully present with another person in your professional world. Note what happens in the conversation that would not have happened if you had been managing it toward a result.
  • Within 14 Days: Complete the AI amplifier action from Exercise 7. Hand one specific task to AI and redirect the freed time to one specific depth-level activity. Track the difference in how you feel at the end of that day versus a day where the same time went to production.
  • Day 30: Return to your scorecard in Exercise 4. Rate yourself again. Has any dimension moved? What does the evidence from the 30 days show about where your depth is developing — and where it still needs investment?

Before You Close This

The participant in my workshop was right. The most dangerous thing happening right now is not that people are using AI. It is that people are using AI to present a version of themselves that does not exist — and in doing so, are building their professional lives on borrowed competence that has a moment of reckoning attached to it.

But that warning is only half the picture. And if you have done this worksheet honestly — if you have audited your utility, traced your formation, mapped your dimensions, and written a purpose statement in your own voice — you have moved into the other half. The half where the X-ray is not a threat but an invitation. The half where AI frees up space for the thing that makes you irreplaceable rather than covering the gap where it should be.

You are not here to produce. You are here to bring your specific, singular, irreplaceable human depth — your judgment, your connection, your intuition, your character, your capacity to be with — to the people and the work that need exactly what you, and only you, can offer. That is not an aspiration. It is a description of what was already true before the algorithm arrived. The algorithm simply made it impossible to ignore.

The untaught lesson of purpose is this: your meaning was never in your utility. It was always in your depth. Go and live from that depth. Every day. In every conversation, every decision, every moment of genuine connection. Not because the algorithm cannot compete with it — though it cannot. But because it is the most alive thing you can do with the one life you have been given.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know about your own purpose that you did not know when you opened it.

The output-based identity can be released. The depth-based identity can be built. The five dimensions can be developed — one deliberate, daily, depth-level act at a time. The algorithm made the gap visible. You have named it. Now go and close it — not by running faster on the machine's treadmill, but by going deeper into the dimensions of yourself the machine cannot touch.

Go deeper. Every day.
