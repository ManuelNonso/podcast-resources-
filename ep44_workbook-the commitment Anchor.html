Part One

Diagnose

Before you can design the anchor, you need to diagnose the gap precisely. Part One identifies the specific outcomes where intention has been losing to inertia — and names the real obstacle behind each one.

The Unanchored Intention Inventory
Mapping the outcomes where intention has been consistently losing to inertia.

List up to five outcomes you have been intending — genuinely intending — that have not yet been produced. For each one, note how long it has been waiting, and what the most recent missed deadline was. Be specific. Not "I want to grow my business" — "I have not yet recorded Episode 1 of my podcast / sent the proposal / launched the offer."

The duration is not evidence of a motivation problem. It is evidence of an anchor problem. The outcomes above are the ones you will build anchors for in Part Two.

Anchor Design by Obstacle Type

Friction of beginning → anchor that puts the desired action physically in motion before the resistance arrives (the soaked cloth). Fear of imperfection or failure → anchor that makes proceeding less costly than not proceeding (the subscription). Fear of visibility → anchor that puts you on record with one specific person whose opinion you value. No external accountability → anchor that creates a public or relational stake in completing.

Part Three

Deploy

Designed anchors that are not deployed are just intentions with better descriptions. Part Three creates the deployment plan — the specific actions you take today — and pressure-tests each anchor against the moments most likely to dislodge it.

3
7
The Anchor Pressure Test
Testing each anchor against the moments most likely to dislodge it.

A well-designed anchor should hold even when you are tired, busy, distracted, or when the old inertia reasserts itself. Test each of your three anchors against these scenarios before you deploy them.

🎯 Test One — The Low-Energy Day

It has been a hard day. You are tired. The last thing you feel like doing is the action your anchor is designed to produce. The non-negotiable activity occurs — and the anchor is there, visible, ready to activate. What happens?

If the honest answer is "I would probably ignore the anchor and deal with it later" — the anchor needs redesigning. The micro-commitment must create a cost that is higher than the relief of ignoring it on a hard day.
🎯 Test Two — The Busy Week

Everything is urgent. The week is full. There are twelve other things demanding your attention at exactly the moment the anchor activates. Does the anchor hold — or does the busyness provide cover for delay?

The non-negotiable anchor point is your protection here. If it is genuinely non-negotiable, the busyness of the week does not prevent it from occurring. The anchor activates regardless.
🎯 Test Three — The "Good Enough" Reason

A very good reason to delay arrives. Not laziness — a genuine, credible, almost irresistible reason why now is not the right moment. The kind of reason that has worked before. Does the anchor hold?

This is the test the software subscription was designed to pass. The "cancel anytime" clause was supposed to be the exit. When it closed, the anchor held — because the cost of not launching exceeded the cost of the reason to delay. Does your anchor create an equivalent asymmetry?
The Asymmetry Principle

The Commitment Anchor works because it creates an asymmetry: the cost of not acting (financial, relational, practical, or physical) becomes higher than the cost of acting. When this asymmetry exists, the good enough reason cannot win. When it does not, the reason always can. Every anchor you design should create a clear asymmetry — where proceeding is the cheaper option.

Part Four

Sustain

Before You Close This

You have just done something most people never do: you have moved from intention to design. You have named the outcomes, diagnosed the obstacles, identified the non-negotiables, built the anchors, pressure-tested them, and committed to their deployment.

The cloth is soaking in the basin. The subscription is paid. The action that follows is not a matter of willpower. It is a matter of when the non-negotiable next occurs — which is today.

Before you close this session — write the one insight from this design process that you most need to remember when the old inertia returns. The one thing that, if you held it clearly in that moment, would make the anchor hold.

The anchor does not move the ship. It prevents drift. You have now set three anchors across three areas of your life where drift has been the enemy. The outcomes are no longer dependent on the weather of your motivation. They are held in place by the design of your commitment.

Set the anchor. Let the ship stay where you have decided it should be.
