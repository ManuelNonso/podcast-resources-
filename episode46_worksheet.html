Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is not a reflection exercise to be skimmed. It is a coaching session on paper. It is designed to be completed in a single sitting — with your full attention, without interruption, and with the same honesty you would bring to a live session.

The four parts move in sequence. Each builds on what the one before it uncovered. Do not skip ahead. Do not filter your answers for how they sound — write what is actually true, in the language your body uses rather than the language your mind edits.

The coaching questions in purple are the questions I use in live sessions. Treat them as if you are sitting across from me. Pause before answering. The first answer is rarely the real one.

Everything you write here is private. The only person who needs to see it is you.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the belief that has been quietly running your outcomes, in the precise language it actually uses
  • Something traced — the origin of that belief, and the evidence it has been gathering to support itself
  • Something separated — a clear distinction between who you are and what you have been carrying
  • Something committed — one specific action that belongs to the person you actually are, taken before you close this worksheet
Part One

Discover — The Three-Layer Truth

Before you can change what you are carrying, you have to see it clearly. Part One takes you through the three layers of how you have been describing your problem — from what you say in public to what the core fear actually is. Most people have never reached layer three in writing. That is where this session begins.

1
The Three Layers — Name Your Real Problem
Moving from what you say publicly to what you are actually afraid is true.

Think about the area of your life where you feel most stuck right now — the thing you keep starting, or avoiding, or returning to. It might be a business, a career move, a relationship pattern, a creative project, or a goal that keeps stalling. Hold it in mind as you answer each layer below.

Coaching Prompt — Layer One

What do you say about this problem when someone asks how things are going? The version you are comfortable sharing with anyone.

This is your public layer. Write it exactly as you say it — don't improve it.

Coaching Prompt — Layer Two

What do you say about this same problem to someone you trust? The version with a little more edge. More honesty. What do you admit privately that you would not say in a room full of people?

Notice if there is more vulnerability here. There should be.

Coaching Prompt — Layer Three

Now go deeper. Underneath the private admission, what are you actually afraid is true about you? Not about the situation — about you. What is the belief that your struggles have been quietly confirming? Write it in the exact language it uses in your body — not the polished version, the real one.

Take your time. If it feels uncomfortable to write — that discomfort is data. That is the layer we are working with today.

Notice ThisThe gap between Layer One and Layer Three is exactly the distance between the problem you have been managing and the problem that has actually been running the show. Everything you do from this point forward in this session is directed at Layer Three.

2
The Root Audit — Where Did This Belief Begin?
Tracing the Layer Three belief to its origin — before it became invisible.

A belief this deeply embedded did not arrive last year. It was assembled over time — from experiences, from the people who had authority over you, from moments where reaching for something important resulted in a loss that felt significant. This exercise traces the root.

🌳 The Root Analogy — Hold This

Think about a tree that keeps producing defective fruit. You cut the branches. You strip the leaves. You change the soil. But the problem keeps returning — because it is in the root, underground, invisible from the surface.

Your limiting belief is the root. Everything visible — the decisions, the hesitations, the patterns, the results — are branches. Until you name and address the root, you will keep treating symptoms.

Coaching Prompt

When did you first absorb this belief? Not necessarily in those exact words — but when did you first receive the message, in experience or in feeling, that the thing you fear is true?

It may not be a single moment. It may be a pattern across several years. Name what comes first.

Coaching Prompt

Think about the last time you started something new. What was happening in your body in the days before you began — not just the excitement, but the feeling underneath it? Was there a kind of quiet bracing? A low hum of preparing for things not to work?

Not what you thought. What you felt. Where did you feel it?

3
The Evidence Architecture — How Your Belief Built Its Case
Mapping the table and its legs — and identifying what has been left off the table entirely.

Your mind has been building an evidence base for this belief for years. This exercise makes that architecture visible — and then begins the process of dismantling it.

🪑 The Table Analogy — Hold This

Tony Robbins describes beliefs as a table. The belief is the tabletop. And holding it up are legs — every piece of evidence the mind has gathered to confirm it. A failure. A rejection. A moment of public embarrassment. A time someone confirmed what you feared was already true.

The mind privileges evidence that confirms what it already believes, and files contradictory evidence under "exceptions." To destroy the belief, you pull the legs away — and replace them immediately with new ones built from evidence that is already true.

Coaching Prompt

Now I want you to look for what your mind has been ignoring. What evidence exists in your own life — right now, already true — that directly contradicts this belief? Not what you hope might happen. What has already happened that your mind filed under "exception" or "luck" or "that doesn't count"?

This is where most people find more than they expected. Look for it honestly.

Part Two

Define — Build the New Belief

Removing a limiting belief without immediately replacing it leaves a vacuum. The mind reaches back for what it knows. Part Two builds the accurate alternative — not a wishful statement, but a belief with real legs, constructed from evidence that is already true in your own life.

4
The Separation — Who You Are vs What You Have Been Carrying
The most important distinction in this entire session.

A limiting belief that has been gathering evidence for years becomes indistinguishable from identity. It stops feeling like a story you picked up somewhere and starts feeling like a fact about who you are. This exercise separates the two — clearly, and on paper.

"You are not the sum of what has not worked. You are the whole of everything you have built, learned, survived, and chosen."
Coaching Prompt — The Judgment Question

If someone else brought you the complete evidence of your own life — everything you have done, every time you showed up, every moment you kept going despite the fear — and asked you to judge whether that person was fundamentally someone who fails, or someone who is still learning — what would you say?

Answer as if the person is not you. With the same generosity and accuracy you would give a client or a close friend.

5
The New Table — Build Your Empowering Belief
A belief with real legs — constructed from evidence that already exists, not from what you hope might become true.

The new belief is not an affirmation you repeat until it feels true. It is a structured claim — a tabletop held up by legs that are already standing. Written here, with the evidence beneath it, so it can be called on every time the old belief tries to reassemble.

💧 The Source Analogy — Hold This

A town with a contaminated water source installs brand new pipes. Modern. Efficient. The water looks clearer. But the source has not changed. Everything that flows through those beautiful new pipes still comes from the same toxic place.

A new plan, new strategy, or new beginning does not change the source. The source is the belief. Change the source first — and then let everything flow from it.

Coaching Prompt

Based on the evidence you have named — what is the more accurate belief about who you actually are? Write it as a statement, not a hope. Present tense. Already true. Built on what your life has already demonstrated.

This is your new tabletop. The legs go underneath it.

Part Three

Pressure Test — Hold the New Belief Under Fire

A belief that only holds in calm conditions is not yet strong enough to carry you through the moments that matter. Part Three tests the new belief against the scenarios where the old belief is most likely to surge back. This is where the structure is stress-tested before it needs to perform.

6
Scenario One — When the Old Pattern Shows Up Again
The moment of failure, setback, or unwanted result — and what you do with it now.

The old belief will find moments to surge. Not continuously — but at specific trigger points. The clearest trigger is any result that resembles a previous failure. A deal that falls through. A launch that underperforms. A conversation that goes badly. In that moment, the old belief will move quickly to gather this new event as evidence.

⚡ Pressure Test Scenario

You are six months into your new beginning. Something goes wrong — a significant setback, a result that mirrors a previous failure, a moment where the old story would have said "see, I told you." The feeling in your body is familiar. The old belief is moving toward the table.

7
Scenario Two — When Success Arrives and the Old Belief Dismisses It
The equally dangerous moment — when a win is filed as "exception" or "luck" before it can become evidence.

The old belief does not only attack during failure. It also works during success — by convincing you that a positive result does not count. "That was luck." "That will not last." "They do not really know what they are getting." This exercise addresses that equally powerful undermining mechanism.

⚡ Pressure Test Scenario

Something goes well. A meaningful result. A deal closes. A piece of work lands powerfully. Someone offers you significant praise or recognition. The old belief stirs — not with attack, but with dismissal. "That was a fluke." "Wait until they find out." "This will not continue."

Coaching Prompt

Think about how many compliments you received today compared to the one piece of critical feedback that stayed with you. The brain is biased toward the negative — not as a character flaw, but as a survival mechanism. Your job now is to consciously rebalance that ratio. What is one win from the last 30 days that deserves to be a leg under your new table?

Part Four

Protect — The Structure That Makes This Durable

The insight from a coaching session has a half-life. Without structure around it, the most penetrating realisation fades within days as the familiar patterns reassert themselves. Part Four builds the practice and the commitment that turn today's clarity into a new permanent operating level.

8
The 30-Day Practice Schedule
The structure that builds a new neuropathway rather than a temporary feeling.

Tony Robbins describes beliefs as neuropathways — grooves worn into the brain by repetition. The old belief has years of repetition behind it. The new one has this session. What closes that gap is consistent, intentional repetition over the next 30 days — not effort, structure.

  • Today — Before You Close This Worksheet: Read your new empowering belief out loud. Then take the Gap Action named in your commitment contract below. Not tomorrow. Before this session ends.
  • Days 1 to 14 — Every Morning: Read your new belief before your phone, before your email, before your feed. Not as a wish — as a recalibration. The belief you start the day from is the one that makes decisions on your behalf. Choose it consciously.
  • Within 7 Days: Name the Layer Three belief — the one you wrote in Exercise 1 — out loud to one person you trust. Use the exact language from this workbook. Say it out loud. External naming is part of the process. Kept silent, beliefs retain more power.
  • Day 14: Return to Exercise 3 (The Evidence Architecture). Add every new piece of evidence that has accumulated in the two weeks since this session. What happened — in work, in relationships, in your own internal experience — that belongs to the new table?
  • Day 30: Return to Exercise 1 (The Three Layers). Run the full audit again. How has the language of the three layers changed? Has the Layer Three belief shifted — or revealed something beneath it? The work is iterative. The layers come in sequence.
  • Any Time the Old Belief Surges: Apply the Pressure Test protocol from Exercise 6. Ask the question. Read the new belief. Name one piece of evidence from your table. Then take the smallest possible action that belongs to the person the new belief describes — before the surge subsides.
  • Day 90: Revisit this entire worksheet. What has changed? What has the new belief predicted that has now come true? What have you done that the old belief would not have allowed? Name it in writing. Let the evidence accumulate on the right table.
9
The Gap Action — The Decision That Must Happen Today
The new belief must be lived before it is felt. This is where it begins.

A new identity that is only held intellectually does not become real until it is acted from. The gap action is the specific decision or move that belongs to the person your new belief describes — taken today, before the old belief has the chance to talk you out of it.

Coaching Prompt

What is the one decision, move, or action you have been delaying — that the old belief has been blocking — that the person your new belief describes would take today? Not the biggest thing on your list. The one that the old belief has specifically been using its fear to prevent.

Name it precisely. Vague gap actions do not get taken.

Before You Close This

You have just done something most people never do. You have sat with the fear that has been following you from one beginning to the next — named it at its root, traced where it came from, audited the evidence it has been using, and replaced it with a belief built from what your life has already demonstrated.

The old belief is not gone. It has years of neuropathway behind it. It will try to reassemble. That is not failure — that is how the brain works. Your job now is to keep choosing the new table. To keep adding legs to it. To let the evidence accumulate on the right side.

You are not the sum of what has not worked. You are the whole of everything you have built, learned, survived, and chosen. That is who goes into the next chapter. That is who you signed for just now.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know that you did not know when you opened it. Not the most useful insight. The thing that cannot be unfelt.

The weight you were carrying had a name. The belief had legs that can be pulled away. And underneath all of it is someone with far more evidence of capability than the old story was willing to admit.
