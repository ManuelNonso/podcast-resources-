Before You Begin

How to Use This Worksheet

This is the coaching session the episode prepared you for, in written form, taken further than a single listening allows. Four parts. Nine exercises. One honest diagnosis, and one deliberate interpretation, chosen and acted on by the end of it.

This worksheet is not about phones. It is about the meaning you have been quietly assigning to a circumstance you did not fully choose, whether that circumstance lives in your health, your career, a relationship, your finances, your upbringing, or your own sense of identity. Pick the one shaping your life the most right now, and work that one all the way through.

From Part Two onward, this worksheet branches into two paths, circumstances you can still change, and circumstances that are fixed or already in the past, because they call for different work. Read both branches before choosing. Many people find they've been treating a changeable circumstance as fixed, or a fixed one as something to keep fighting.

🌧️ The Rain and the Garden — Hold This Throughout

Rain falling on two gardens is neutral. It does not decide anything on its own. One garden, with good soil and proper drainage already in place, takes that same rain and produces growth. Another, without that preparation, takes the same rain and floods.

The rain never changes between the two gardens. What was already in the ground to receive it does. This worksheet is how you examine, and where necessary rebuild, the ground that receives whatever falls on you next.

What This Session Will Produce
  • Something named — the specific circumstance and the exact meaning you have been quietly assigning to it, in the domain of life it actually affects most
  • Something traced — the moment, voice, or comparison that first planted this interpretation in you
  • Something separated — the plain facts of your circumstance, pulled apart from the story you built on top of them
  • Something committed — one deliberate interpretation, and one visible action built from it, taken within the next seven days
Part One

Discover — Naming the Meaning Currently Running Your Life

Before you can choose a meaning on purpose, you need to see clearly which meaning is already running the show, where it came from, and whether the circumstance underneath it can still be changed.

1
The Domain Scan
Naming the specific circumstance, and the meaning already attached to it.

This exercise begins with an honest inventory across the areas of life where an unquestioned meaning tends to hide the longest.

Coaching Prompt

For each domain below, name one circumstance you didn't fully choose, and the meaning you've been carrying about what it says about you or what is possible for you.

Write the meaning as a full sentence, not a single word. "It's unfair" is a feeling. "It's unfair, and that means I'll always be behind people who had it easier" is a meaning you can actually work with.

Notice ThisThe domain where you wrote the fastest, most rehearsed sentence is often the one where the meaning has been running longest, unquestioned.

2
The Origin — Tracing Where the Meaning Was Planted
Choose the one domain costing you the most, and find where this interpretation started.

Look back at your four answers. Choose the one you will work through for the rest of this worksheet, the one shaping the most decisions in your life right now.

Coaching Prompt

When did you first arrive at this meaning? Was there a specific moment, a specific voice, or a specific comparison to someone else that planted this exact interpretation in you?

Take your time. For some, this traces to childhood. For others, it's more recent, a single hard season generalized into a permanent story.

Notice ThisThe meaning you found is not evidence of poor character or weak thinking. It was a reasonable conclusion, drawn from limited information, at a moment when you likely had far less power than you do now.

3
The Diagnosis — Changeable or Fixed
Naming plainly whether the underlying circumstance can still be acted on.

This diagnosis determines which path you'll build in Part Two. Getting it right matters more than getting it fast.

Coaching Prompt

Setting the meaning aside for a moment, look only at the circumstance itself. Is there any real action available to you that would change it going forward, or has it already happened, or is it genuinely outside your control?

Be honest rather than hopeful or defeatist. Some circumstances are genuinely fixed, like a childhood or a past event. Others feel fixed but actually have real room to move.

"You cannot always choose what happens to you. You can always choose what you decide it means, and that repeated choice is what actually builds a life."

5
The Words — Drafting What You'll Tell Yourself
Preparing the sentence for the moment the old meaning tries to return.

The old meaning built itself over months or years. It will not disappear the moment you write a new one. Writing your response now, while you're clear headed, makes it far more available when you actually need it.

Coaching Prompt

What is the one sentence that reminds you the old meaning was a reasonable conclusion once, but is not the only honest reading available to you now?

Write it the way you would actually say it to yourself, not the way it would sound on a poster.

Read it back out loud once before moving on. If it sounds like something you'd tell a client or a friend you love, it's ready.

Part Three

Pressure Test — Testing the New Meaning Against Real Life

A new meaning survives easily on paper. It does not survive as easily under proof of the old story, a hard week, or someone else's pushback, unless you've already thought it through. Part Three does that thinking now.

6
Scenario One — Someone Proves the Old Excuse Wrong
When someone with a similar circumstance builds what you assumed was impossible.
The Scenario

You come across someone with a remarkably similar circumstance to yours, who has built something you had quietly assumed was impossible for someone in your position. The instinct is either to dismiss it as luck, or to feel worse about your own situation.

7
Scenario Two — A Hard Week Brings the Old Story Back
When exhaustion makes the old, familiar meaning feel true again.
The Scenario

You've been living from your new interpretation for a couple of weeks. Then a genuinely hard week arrives, and the old meaning, the familiar one, quietly starts feeling true again because it's easier to fall into than to hold something new under pressure.

8
Scenario Three — Someone Calls Your New Meaning Naive
When your new interpretation meets real social pushback.
The Scenario

You share your new interpretation with someone close to you, and instead of support, you get skepticism. They call it naive, or tell you that you're just avoiding the truth of how unfair your circumstance really was.

Part Four

Protect — Making This a Practice, Not a Moment

One new interpretation is a good start. It is not the whole shift. Part Four turns this worksheet into an ongoing practice, so the next unquestioned meaning doesn't get to run quietly for years before you notice it.

9
The Weekly Practice — Building the Habit
The simple, recurring practice that catches an unquestioned meaning early next time.

The goal is not to fix one meaning. The goal is to build the habit of noticing quickly when you're living inside an interpretation you never actually chose.

  • Within 7 Days: Take the first build move, or say the reframe statement out loud to someone, from Exercise 4.
  • The Day After: Write down what actually happened, and compare it honestly to what the old meaning predicted would happen.
  • Every Week: Ask yourself one question. "What am I explaining right now instead of building from?"
  • Every Month: Revisit your Domain Scan from Exercise 1. Has a new unquestioned meaning started forming somewhere else? Name it while it's still fresh.
  • Whenever Proof Shows Up: Run the reminder from Exercise 6 before letting evidence that contradicts your old story get explained away.

Before You Close This

My wife and a lady we know bought the exact same phone. Same make, same brand, weeks apart. One ended up with a screensaver of our family and a phone shaped entirely around staying connected to the people she loves. The other built something different, with the same object, because of what she brought to it. The phone never chose either outcome. They did.

Whatever circumstance you worked through today, the facts were never going to change simply by waiting, and they don't need to change for you to build something real from them. The meaning moves the moment you name it precisely, question whether it's the only honest reading available, and choose, on purpose, the one that lets you build.

Living in Full Expression means owning the meaning you make of what happens to you, whether or not the circumstance itself was ever fair.

Before you close this session — write the one thing you now know, that you did not know when you opened it.

The circumstance will still be there tomorrow whether you name the meaning or not. You now know exactly what it is, exactly whether it can still change, and exactly what your first honest move looks like. The only thing left is the seven days.

Choose your meaning before it chooses your life.
Live in full expression.
